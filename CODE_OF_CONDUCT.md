# Code of conduct

This project collects evidence about politically contested events. It will attract people who disagree with its findings, and it should — that is the test of whether it is worth anything.

## The standard

- **Argue with the data.** Point at a record, a source, a coding decision. That is always welcome, including when it damages the project's headline number.
- **No personal attacks**, no speculation about contributors' motives, no harassment in issues, PRs or discussions.
- **No brigading.** Organised campaigns to flood the tracker, whether to add unsupported cases or to discredit the dataset, will be closed without discussion.
- **Assume good faith on the first exchange, and evidence after that.**

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, close or lock any issue, PR or comment that breaches this. Repeated breaches result in a block. Decisions are made in public where possible, and the reason is stated.

## The specific hazard here

This project touches gun policy, and gun policy arguments are frequently conducted in bad faith on both sides. Two failure modes get people removed fastest:

1. **Submitting cases you have not read a source for**, because the story "sounds right".
2. **Demanding records be removed** because they are politically inconvenient rather than because they are wrong.

The first corrupts the data. The second corrupts the project. Corrections with a source are always accepted; deletions without one never are.

## Scope

This applies to project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, and any space where you are representing the project. It does not govern your own accounts or your own writing elsewhere.
